Collecting Unpaid Rent with Yardi: How to Recover Written-Off Resident Balances

Yardi is very good at moving a resident out and writing off what they owe. What happens to that balance next is up to you. This guide covers how Voyager and Breeze handle former-resident balances, what Yardi's Collections API does and doesn't confirm, how to place an account with no integration at all, and how to post the money when it comes back.

Shawn Juarez, Managing Director, US Collections and Growth · LinkedIn · Published October 29, 2026 · 12 min read
Quick answer

Yardi handles the move-out accounting and the write-off; collecting the balance is up to you or a collection partner. Voyager has a MITS-based Collections API that lets an approved third party receive resident, lease and balance data and documents over SFTP (Yardi). Yardi doesn't publicly confirm that payments or status updates post back. Breeze has no published collections API. You don't need an integration to place an account: export the pre-write-off ledger, the move-out statement and the lease, send them securely, and post recoveries gross, with the commission as a separate expense.

What Yardi Does With a Former Resident's Balance

Yardi Voyager handles the move-out accounting and the write-off. Collecting the balance after that is left to you or to whoever you place it with. Yardi describes Voyager Residential as one platform that combines property and financial data and includes move-out management (Yardi Voyager Residential; Voyager brochure). In practice, the move-out sequence most multifamily teams run in Voyager looks like this:

  1. Change the resident to past status.
  2. Run deposit accounting: apply the security deposit, final prorations, and damage or cleaning charges.
  3. Capture the forwarding address.
  4. Post the accounting and print the itemized move-out statement.
  5. Write off what's left, once you've decided it won't be paid in-house.

Final utility bills can land in the same step. Yardi's utility billing product folds the final bill into deposit accounting and posts it to the resident's ledger before the deposit is returned (Yardi Utility Billing). The name of the resident-facing document varies by setup: "move-out statement," "final account statement" and "security deposit disposition" all show up, so use whatever your database prints.

The write-off is an accounting entry, not a decision about whether the debt exists. Voyager's charge write-off creates a reversing transaction and a receipt for it, filtered by property, resident, charge code and date (Voyager help, public copy), and Yardi's 2026 training says residential write-offs use system-defined charge-code mappings (Yardi YASC 2026). Which bad-debt or allowance accounts get hit depends on your own charge-code and general ledger setup. The practical lesson: save the ledger before you write it off, because the write-off changes how the balance looks, and the pre-write-off ledger is what a collector needs.

Voyager vs. Breeze for Collections

Voyager and Breeze both handle move-outs and write-offs, but only Voyager has a published collections interface. Breeze is built to keep outside integrations to a minimum (Yardi Breeze), so Breeze users should plan on a report-and-export workflow.

The Voyager 8 Collections Dashboard is a delinquency tool for current residents. The Yardi Collections API: What's Confirmed and What Isn't

Yardi's standard connector for collections is the Collections API, based on the MITS data standard. Here is what Yardi publicly says it does:

The same listing says lease documents and other files can be sent over SFTP. That confirms an outbound placement channel. Yardi does not publish the API's field list, schedule or status map, so treat the rest as something to confirm in writing:

  • Automated placement: partly confirmed. Voyager tracks collections history per resident and the API can send the account, but whether placement is instant, batched or manually released depends on setup.
  • Payments posted back to the ledger: not confirmed for the Collections API. Yardi confirms automatic ledger posting for its own Payment Processing, which is a different product (Yardi Payment Processing).
  • Status updates back to Voyager: not confirmed publicly.
  • Recalling a placed account: not confirmed publicly. Don't confuse this with reversing an ACH payment, which Yardi's payment terms do cover (Yardi payment terms).

Cost is also less clear than it looks. Yardi's interface partner program charges the partner an annual license fee per interface, sometimes transaction-based, and requires a data exchange agreement, development against a Voyager sandbox and a pilot client before general release (Yardi Interface Partner Program). It doesn't say what you, the property manager, pay for the module or setup, so check your Yardi order form. Current setup guides live in Yardi Client Central (Client Central). An interface is convenient, but it isn't required.

How to Place a Yardi Balance Without an Integration

You don't need the Collections API to send a balance to collections. Every Voyager and Breeze database can produce what a collector actually works from: the resident's identity, the lease, the final statement and the ledger. The export just has to be complete and sent securely.

  1. Wait out the deposit deadline. Send the itemized statement first. Placement doesn't replace your state's deposit accounting (see our guide to security deposits and move-out charges).
  2. Export the pre-write-off ledger. Run the resident ledger before the write-off posts, or filter to show the original charges.
  3. Print the move-out statement exactly as it was sent, with the date it went out.
  4. Pull the lease and addenda from the resident's documents, plus the application if it holds guarantor or employer details.
  5. Note the itemization date you can support: usually the date of the final statement (more on that below).
  6. Flag anything that stops collection: disputes, bankruptcy, a deceased resident, active military, or an attorney representing the resident.
  7. Send by SFTP or a secure portal, never as an ordinary email attachment.

Our list of documents a collection agency needs goes through each item, and our guide on when to send unpaid rent to collections covers timing. Sending the account is half the job. The other half is recording what comes back.

Posting Recoveries Back to Yardi: Gross vs. Net

Record recoveries gross. Post the full amount the former resident paid, show the collection commission as its own expense, and let the cash line match the bank deposit. Posting only the net check understates both what you recovered and what recovery cost you.

Recording a $1,000 recovery with a $300 commission
LineAmountHow to record it
Gross recovery$1,000Reinstate the written-off receivable, or record a recovery, for the full payment
Commission$300Collection expense, per owner policy
Cash$700Matches the remittance deposit
Resident balanceDown $1,000Reduced by what the resident paid, not by the cash you received

Under the allowance method, standard accounting reinstates the receivable first and then records the cash against it (OpenStax, Principles of Financial Accounting). Add the commission debit so the credit to the resident's receivable equals the gross payment. For tax, the IRS treats a recovered bad debt you deducted earlier as income to the extent the deduction gave you a tax benefit (IRS Publication 334). Have your controller sign off on the mapping, because management agreements and owner reports differ.

Each month, match four numbers: gross payments the collector reports, recovery receipts posted in Yardi, commission posted to expense, and net deposits in the bank reconciliation. If those tie out, owners can trust the recovery line. Our guide to how asset managers measure bad debt recovery covers the KPIs they'll look at.

What a Yardi Placement File Needs for Reg F and Credit Reporting

Regulation F requires a collector to give the former resident validation information, including an itemization from a fixed date to the current balance. The collector picks one of five reference dates: the last statement date, charge-off date, last payment date, transaction date or judgment date, and has to use it consistently afterwards (CFPB, 12 CFR 1006.34). The itemization shows interest, fees, payments and credits since that date, even when a line is zero (CFPB itemization guidance).

For a Yardi account, the final move-out statement date is usually the cleanest choice, as long as you can reproduce that statement and the balance on that date. That's why the pre-write-off ledger matters. Our explainer on the Reg F itemization date goes deeper.

Credit reporting needs more. Metro 2, the industry's reporting format, carries account status, date of first delinquency, balance history and consumer identifiers (CFPB credit reporting white paper; CDIA), and whoever furnishes the data must keep written accuracy procedures and handle disputes under Regulation V (CFPB, 12 CFR 1022). Yardi's public Collections API description doesn't say it produces a Metro 2 file, so the collector has to build that from your export.

Keeping Resident Data Secure When It Leaves Yardi

Yardi's cloud is audited (Yardi says it provides annual SOC 2 and twice-yearly SOC 1 reports, Yardi Cloud Security), but once you export a file, its security depends on you and the recipient. The FTC Safeguards Rule expressly covers collection agencies and requires encryption, multifactor authentication, access controls and oversight of service providers (FTC). A covered business must also tell the FTC within 30 days of discovering a breach of unencrypted data affecting 500 or more people (FTC, 2024).

  • Send files by SFTP or a secure portal, never as plain email attachments.
  • Send only the fields and documents needed to collect.
  • Ask the recipient for a current SOC 2 Type II report or equivalent.
  • Put retention, deletion and breach-notice terms in the contract.
  • Log every export and who received it.

How Big Is Yardi in Multifamily?

Yardi is one of the largest multifamily platforms, but there's no audited, current U.S. market share. A Yardi-hosted interview claimed customers represent roughly half the U.S. multifamily market (Yardi Investment Suite), and a 2026 independent count put about 15 million residential units on Yardi across the U.S. and Canada (Propexo). Neither figure is audited. We also found no public data on delinquency or bad debt among Yardi clients, so measure your own: write-offs as a share of gross potential rent, gross and net recovery on placed debt, and days from move-out to placement. Key takeaways
  • A Yardi write-off is an accounting entry, not proof the debt is gone; save the pre-write-off ledger before it posts.
  • Voyager's Collections API sends resident, lease and balance data plus documents over SFTP; payment and status posting back aren't publicly confirmed.
  • Breeze is designed to minimize integrations, so plan on a report-and-export workflow.
  • You can place any Yardi balance without an integration if the file is complete and sent securely.
  • Post recoveries gross: the full payment against the resident, the commission as an expense, and cash matching the deposit.
  • The final move-out statement date is usually the cleanest Regulation F itemization date for a Yardi account.
  • No public data shows Yardi clients' delinquency or recovery rates, so track your own.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • Does Yardi have a built-in collections module?

    Voyager tracks collections history on the resident record, Voyager 8 adds a Collections Dashboard for delinquency, and the Collections API sends placed accounts to a third party. Yardi itself doesn't act as a collection agency for former-resident balances.

  • Does Yardi Breeze integrate with collection agencies?

    Not through any published collections API. Yardi says Breeze is built to minimize outside integrations, so Breeze users usually place accounts by exporting the ledger, final statement and lease.

  • Does writing off a balance in Yardi mean the resident no longer owes it?

    No. A Voyager write-off creates a reversing accounting transaction. It changes how the balance shows in receivables, not whether the former resident legally owes it.

  • Is Yardi Debt Manager used for resident debt?

    No. Yardi Debt Manager manages real estate loans and investment debt for owners. It has nothing to do with unpaid rent or former-resident balances.

  • Can a former resident still pay through RentCafe after move-out?

    Sometimes. It depends on whether the account stays payable and portal-enabled in your setup. Yardi doesn't publicly confirm post-move-out portal access, so check your configuration.

  • Who pays for the Yardi Collections API?

    Yardi charges interface partners an annual license fee per interface, sometimes transaction-based. What you pay for the module or setup isn't published, so check your Yardi order form and the partner's agreement.

  • Can I email a collections file exported from Yardi?

    Avoid it. Send resident files by SFTP or a secure portal. The FTC Safeguards Rule covers collection agencies and expects encryption, access controls and oversight of service providers.

  • Is there published data on delinquency among Yardi clients?

    No public dataset reports resident delinquency or recovery rates for Voyager or Breeze clients. Yardi Matrix publishes multifamily loan delinquency, which measures owners' mortgages, not residents' rent.
