Not the best fit for: commercial tenants, which need a commercial collection agency.

Check before placing: its active Oregon collection agency registration on NMLS Consumer Access. Ours is on file; ask us for our NMLS ID, along with the sample notice and report described in the vetting section below.

2. Assurant Recovery Solutions: Best for large multifamily portfolios on Yardi

Assurant Recovery Solutions, formerly RentCollect Global, is the multifamily debt-recovery arm of Assurant, the Fortune 500 insurance company. 3. RD Fuller: Best for first-party collection before you place accounts

RD Fuller, founded in Denver in 2010 and now operating from Cincinnati, runs a centralized first-party service for multifamily: it works balances in the property's name before they're written off, through a portal where former residents can pay or set up payment plans, at what it calls a reduced contingency fee. Accounts that need third-party collection or litigation go out to a downstream network of agencies and law firms. It doesn't publish rates.

Check before placing: which accounts it handles as first party (in your name) versus which go to a network agency, and that the agency receiving Oregon accounts holds an active Oregon registration on NMLS Consumer Access.

An agency isn't always the right tool, though, and a few alternatives cost less for the right balance.

Options That Aren't Collection Agencies

For small balances with a known address, or large ones that are disputed, a court or a lawyer can beat an agency.

  • Credit reporting you handle yourself: some reporting platforms let a landlord report a former tenant's debt to the credit bureaus for a flat fee per debt. They aren't collection agencies, so you handle calls and payments yourself.
  • Placing from your software: AppFolio's Collections Flow, Buildium's Marketplace, Yardi's Collections API and RealPage OneSite's collections interface all send accounts to partner agencies. The partner sets the price and still needs vetting. Our software guide covers each one.
  • Large or disputed balances: the Oregon State Bar's Referral and Information Services connects you with an Oregon lawyer for a reduced-fee first consultation.
  • Small balances where you know the address: Oregon small claims hears claims up to $10,000, but you must make a good-faith effort to collect before filing and say so under oath, and after judgment you send a written demand by certified mail (Oregon Judicial Department). Our small claims vs. collection agency guide compares the two routes.
  • Better files from the start: Multifamily NW offers Oregon rental forms, landlord-tenant law classes and education, which help build a file an agency can work.

Oregon's Rental Market in 2025 and 2026

Oregon's major markets tightened in 2025, but vacancy still varies widely by city. Multifamily NW's Spring 2025 Apartment Report, covering 605 properties and 43,295 units in Oregon and southwest Washington, put Portland Metro vacancy at 5.91%, down from 6.17% a year earlier, with Eugene at 3.56% and Bend improving from 8.05% to 6.02% (Multifamily NW Spring 2025, via HFO). Portland's average market rent edged up to $2.07 a square foot.

Rent increases are also capped. Senate Bill 611 limits most annual increases to the lower of 10% or 7% plus inflation, with exemptions for buildings under 15 years old (SB 611, 2023). That matters for collections, because "unpaid rent" that includes an unlawful or badly noticed increase isn't collectible. Our city guides for Portland and Salem cover the local courts. How to Vet Any Collection Agency in Oregon

Use these checks on every agency above, including us. Each one tests something an agency's sales page can't prove.

Check Oregon Registration on NMLS Before Anything Else

Oregon requires registration and a bond, and you can verify both in a few minutes.

Under ORS 697.031, no one may operate as a collection agency in Oregon without registering with the Department of Consumer and Business Services. The Division of Financial Regulation takes applications through NMLS, charges a $350 registration fee and requires a bond.

Search the agency's exact legal entity on NMLS Consumer Access, which DFR points consumers to for license status, and get its NMLS ID. Another state's license doesn't count. In July 2026, DFR entered a consent order against a Wisconsin-based collector over alleged unregistered collection activity in Oregon (Sheppard Mullin, 2026).

Also ask whether the firm collects as your agent or buys the debt. A debt buyer needs a separate Oregon debt buyer license, and a firm acting as both needs both (Oregon DFR). Our Oregon rent collection law guide covers the rest of the state rules.

The Agency Should Know What Oregon Lets It Collect

A good Oregon agency screens your file before the first letter and tells you what it won't collect.

Most collection risk on rent debt comes from collecting something the lease or the statute doesn't support. An agency that knows Oregon's rules will ask about each of these when it reviews your file. One that takes every balance as given is leaving the risk with you, because the claim is still yours.

Federal Compliance Should Be on Paper, Not Just Promised

Every agency says it's FDCPA compliant. Ask it to show you how.

Oregon agencies follow the federal FDCPA and Regulation F on top of state law. Those rules are specific enough to test. Ask for these four things before you sign:

  • A sample validation notice. Regulation F requires the debt's itemization, current balance and dispute rights in the first communication or within five days of it (12 CFR 1006.34). Check that the itemization date and balance match your ledger.
  • Its call-frequency rule. A collector is presumed compliant if it places no more than seven calls about a debt in seven days and doesn't call again within seven days after a conversation (CFPB). Ask whether the dialer enforces it automatically.
  • Its email and text opt-out. Every electronic message needs a clear, simple way to opt out (12 CFR 1006.6). See our guide to text and email rules.
  • Its data security. Software placements move resident, lease and balance data, so ask for SOC 2 or equivalent evidence, encryption, breach-notice terms and how data is returned or destroyed when you end the contract.

An agency that hesitates to share these is telling you something. Your residents will see these documents, and so will any regulator who looks at a complaint.

Credit Reporting: Ask How It's Done

Credit reporting helps recovery, but only when the agency handles disputes and corrections properly.

Agencies that report rental debt send it to the bureaus in Metro 2, the industry's standard data format (CDIA). Using the format doesn't make the reporting accurate. The agency is a furnisher under the FCRA and must have written accuracy policies, investigate disputes and correct bad data (12 CFR Part 1022). Before reporting a debt, it must also contact the resident and wait a reasonable period, generally 14 days, for a notice of undeliverability (CFPB).

Ask which bureaus it reports to, the minimum balance and identity data it needs, how it marks a disputed account, and how fast it updates or deletes an account you recall or settle. "Credit impact" language on a sales page isn't proof that an agency reports at all.

How Much Does a Collection Agency Charge in Oregon?

Rental debt is usually worked on contingency. The fee only matters next to the recovery rate.

The National Apartment Association says multifamily collection agencies generally charge nothing up front and keep about 20% to 40% of what they collect, and puts national recovery on rental debt at 15% to 20% of placed balances (NAA). Our fee guide covers what moves the rate.

The useful number is net recovery: what you keep per dollar placed after the agency's fee. At a 20% gross recovery and a 35% fee, you net 13 cents on every dollar placed. A cheaper agency that recovers less can leave you with less.

Speed and File Quality Matter More Than the Pitch

The two things that most change recovery are under your control, so an agency should push you on both.

NAA reports that an account becomes about 16% less likely to be collected for every 30 days it sits unworked, and that agencies can recover up to three times more when a placement includes full Social Security number, date of birth and driver's license data (NAA). An agency that accepts thin files without asking for more, or doesn't push you to place quickly, is leaving money on the table. Our guide on when to send unpaid rent to collections covers the timing.

An Oregon Judgment Faces High Wage Floors

Oregon protects more wages than the federal minimum. Under ORS 18.385, for wages payable on or after July 1, 2026, at least $400 a week or $1,792 a month is exempt, and a creditor takes at most the lesser of 25% of disposable earnings or the amount above those floors. Ask the agency how it values a judgment against those floors and whether it pursues bank accounts or other assets instead. See eviction judgment collection for the wider picture.

Questions to Ask Any Oregon Agency

Use this as your shortlist interview. The answers should come in writing.

  1. What's your exact legal entity name and NMLS ID, and is your Oregon registration active?
  2. Do you collect as our agent, or would you buy the debt? If you buy, do you hold an Oregon debt buyer license?
  3. Which charges on this sample file would you decline to collect under ORS 646.639, and why?
  4. Can I see a sample validation notice and a sample client report?
  5. Do you report to credit bureaus? Which ones, and how do you handle disputes and recalls?
  6. What's every fee you could charge me, including litigation, recalls and integration?
  7. What's your recovery rate on placements like mine, grouped by placement month?
  8. How would you recover on a Oregon judgment, and how fast do you remit what you collect?

Frequently Asked Questions

  • Do collection agencies need to be registered in Oregon?

    Yes. Under ORS 697.031 an agency must register with the Department of Consumer and Business Services through NMLS, pay a $350 fee and keep a $10,000 bond, or $15,000 if it has no Oregon location or trust account. Check the registration on NMLS Consumer Access.

  • Can an Oregon collection agency add fees or interest to a rent balance?

    Only if the rental agreement authorizes them or Oregon law expressly allows them. ORS 646.639 bars collecting any interest, fee or charge beyond that, and late charges must also fit the structures allowed by ORS 90.260.

  • How old can an Oregon rent balance be and still be placed?

    Unpaid rent pursued as a contract debt generally has six years under ORS 12.080, but claims arising under a rental agreement or ORS chapter 90 have one year under ORS 12.125. Which clock applies depends on how the claim is framed, so ask the agency and your counsel before placing older balances.

  • Should I use Oregon small claims court instead of an agency?

    For a balance up to $10,000 where you know where the former resident lives, small claims can work, but you must try to collect in good faith first and send a certified-mail demand after judgment. Oregon's high wage-garnishment floors also limit what a judgment recovers. If the resident has moved, an agency that can locate them is usually faster.

  • Which collection agency is best for Oregon property managers?

    It depends on your portfolio and software. Multifamily and portfolio operators placing former-resident balances fit Elite Recovery Group; large Yardi portfolios fit Assurant Recovery Solutions; Buildium users can connect CACi; RD Fuller suits first-party work before placement. Vet any of them with the checks in this guide.

  • Do I need a different agency for Portland, Salem, Eugene and Bend?

    No. One agency that meets Oregon's requirements can collect statewide. Local rules matter mostly if a balance goes to court, which our Portland and Salem guides cover.

  • Can I pull accounts back from a collection agency?

    Usually yes, if the contract allows recalls. Ask what the recall fee is, if any, and how fast the agency stops collecting and updates the credit bureaus after you recall or a resident disputes. Get both in writing before you place.

  • How long should I give an agency before judging its results?

    Six to twelve months. NAA notes that recovery on a batch of placements takes that long to settle, so ask for results grouped by placement month rather than one blended rate.
