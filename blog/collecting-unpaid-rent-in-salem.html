Collecting Unpaid Rent in Salem, OR: A Property Manager's Guide

Oregon's statewide landlord-tenant act does most of the work in Salem, covered in our Oregon rent collection laws guide. What's local is which county your property is legally in, which court hears the case, and what Salem doesn't have: none of Portland's relocation or screening ordinances. This is general information, not legal advice; confirm current fees with the court before filing.

Shawn Juarez, Managing Director, US Collections and Growth · Published October 8, 2026
Quick answer

Most Salem evictions (FED cases) are filed in Marion County Justice Court. West Salem addresses are legally in Polk County and go to Polk County courts in Dallas, not Marion. Oregon's 2026 fee schedule lists $88 for a residential FED. Small claims, for former-resident balances up to $10,000, cost $57 for claims of $2,500 or less and $102 above that. Salem adds no city relocation-assistance or screening ordinance, so state law alone governs. The practical difference from Portland is lower compliance cost, not a different court process.

Marion County, Polk County, and the West Salem Trap

Salem sits in two counties, and the county, not the city name, decides your court.

For Marion County properties, which is most of Salem, Marion County Justice Court has jurisdiction over FED (forcible entry and detainer) cases, and filings have to be in by 4:30 p.m. West Salem is different: those addresses are physically in Salem but legally in Polk County, so the case goes to Polk County's courts at 850 Main Street in Dallas. Money claims follow the same county split.

Fees are predictable. Timelines are less so.

Eviction and Small-Claims Timelines

State law sets the notice periods, and the local docket sets the pace from there.

Salem adds no local notice on top of Oregon's. After the state notice period runs, the landlord files the FED, and the first hearing, called the initial appearance, is usually set within one to three weeks. Landlord and tenant can sometimes reach an agreement there. If the landlord wins and the tenant doesn't leave, the court issues the notice of restitution and the sheriff enforces it on a scheduled date, which can add several days to a couple of weeks depending on workload.

Small claims take longer. After filing, the hearing is often 30 to 45 days out, service has to be complete before it, and a no-show defendant can be defaulted. Garnishment or execution after judgment adds weeks to months. That gap is why many Salem managers place balances with an agency soon after move-out; our guide to collecting unpaid rent after move-out covers the timing. What Salem leaves out matters as much as what it requires.

What Salem Doesn't Add: No Portland-Style Ordinances

Salem follows state landlord-tenant law without Portland's relocation-assistance or screening ordinances.

Portland requires relocation payments of $2,900 to $4,500 per unit on no-cause terminations, certain non-renewals and rent increases of 10% or more, with its own notice and reporting rules (Portland Housing Bureau). Salem has no equivalent city ordinance and no city-specific screening-criteria rule. Any relocation payment in Salem comes from Oregon's statewide rules for qualifying terminations. We also found no citywide rental inspection or licensing program in the Salem Revised Code; the city handles housing conditions through complaint-driven code enforcement. State law still sets one limit that touches collections directly.

The Rent Cap and Your Balance

You can't raise rent to make up a delinquent balance, and an unlawful increase can become the tenant's defense.

Under ORS 90.323, most annual increases are capped at 7% plus CPI, with a 10% ceiling; the published cap for 2026 is 9.5%. Increases need 90 days' notice, can't happen in the first year of a tenancy, and are limited to once every 12 months. If a tenant claims an increase broke those rules, it can become a defense or counterclaim in the FED or small-claims case, so verify every increase on the ledger before filing. A tenant who can't pay may also be waiting on assistance.

Rental Assistance in Salem

Salem has no large city-run rent fund. Help comes from the county and nonprofits, and it's limited.

  • Marion County Rental Assistance Program serves adults 26 and older with serious and persistent mental illness who are homeless or at risk (Marion County Health).
  • Mid-Willamette Valley Community Action Agency (ARCHES) provides rent relief and eviction prevention, and the city keeps a list of other providers, including the Salvation Army, Family Promise and Northwest Human Services (City of Salem).
  • Salem Housing Authority runs federal programs including Housing Choice Vouchers (Salem Housing Authority).

Federal Emergency Rental Assistance ended its period of performance on September 30, 2025 (U.S. Treasury), so 2026 help is residual state, county and nonprofit money. Eligibility and intake windows change often. The renter base explains some of the local risk.

Salem's Renters: State Workers and Students

A government-heavy renter base is steady but tied to state budget cycles, and the student pocket near downtown turns over fast.

As Oregon's capital, Salem's largest employer is state government, so a meaningful share of renters are paid on state schedules and exposed to budget cycles and reorganizations. That matters when you're verifying income for a payment plan. Near downtown, Willamette University and Chemeketa Community College feed a student market with academic-year leases, summer sublets and co-signers. Put guarantor terms in writing and get forwarding addresses before the end of term, the same discipline that applies anywhere students rent. The market backdrop is softer data.

Salem Market Conditions

Public data for Salem is thin: rent is tracked, vacancy and new supply mostly aren't.

Apartments.com put Salem's average apartment rent at $1,205 a month in October 2026 (Apartments.com, 2026); all-property averages that include single-family homes run higher. No free source publishes a consistent Salem vacancy or delivery series for 2024-2026. On evictions, Oregon recorded 27,290 filings in 2024, the most on record (Oregon Legislature committee record, 2025), and a 2025 records cleanup sealed 6,990 past eviction cases in Marion County and 1,064 in Polk (Salem Reporter, 2025). Those sealed counts show a large historical caseload, not an annual rate. For comparison, Portland's market runs on the same state law at higher cost.

Key takeaways
  • Marion County Justice Court hears FED (eviction) cases for most Salem addresses.
  • West Salem is in Polk County. File there, not in Marion.
  • 2026 fees: $88 to file a residential FED; $57 or $102 for small claims depending on the amount, up to the $10,000 limit.
  • Salem has no Portland-style relocation-assistance or screening ordinance; only state rules apply.
  • Oregon's 2026 rent-increase cap is 9.5%. An increase over the cap can become a defense to your balance.
  • Local rental help is limited and funding-dependent; federal Emergency Rental Assistance ended September 30, 2025.
  • Marion County is one of Oregon's high-volume eviction counties: courts sealed 6,990 past Marion eviction records in one cleanup.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • Which court handles evictions in Salem, Oregon?

    Marion County Justice Court has jurisdiction over FED (eviction) cases for Marion County properties, which covers most of Salem. West Salem properties are in Polk County and go to Polk County's courts in Dallas.

  • How much does it cost to file an eviction in Salem?

    Oregon's 2026 fee schedule lists $88 for a plaintiff filing a residential FED, plus a trial fee if a trial is set. Service and enforcement fees are extra; confirm the justice court's current amounts with the clerk.

  • What is the small-claims limit in Oregon?

    $10,000. Filing costs $57 for claims of $2,500 or less and $102 for claims above $2,500 under the 2026 schedule.

  • Does Salem require relocation assistance like Portland?

    No. Salem has no city relocation-assistance ordinance. Only Oregon's statewide rules apply, which require relocation payments in certain qualifying terminations.

  • How long does an eviction take in Marion County?

    The first appearance is usually set within one to three weeks of filing. If the landlord wins and the tenant stays, the sheriff enforces the judgment on a scheduled date, which adds days to a couple of weeks.

  • Can I raise the rent to help recover a tenant's unpaid balance?

    Not beyond Oregon's cap. For 2026 most increases are capped at 9.5%, with 90 days' notice, once a year and not in the first year. An unlawful increase can become a defense to your rent claim.

  • Is rental assistance still available in Salem?

    Only in limited, funding-dependent forms through Marion County, the Mid-Willamette Valley Community Action Agency and local nonprofits. Federal Emergency Rental Assistance ended September 30, 2025.
