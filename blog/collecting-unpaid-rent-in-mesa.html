Collecting Unpaid Rent in Mesa, Chandler, Gilbert & Tempe

If you manage rentals in Mesa, Chandler, Gilbert or Tempe, the law on unpaid rent is Arizona's. What changes locally is which of the East Valley's justice courts hears the case, what Maricopa County charges, and what drives balances here: ASU leases, build-to-rent homes and heavy new supply. This guide covers those local pieces. It's general information, not legal advice.

Shawn Juarez, Managing Director, US Collections and Growth · LinkedIn · Published October 29, 2026 · 14 min read
Quick answer

File an East Valley eviction in the Maricopa County justice precinct that contains the rental, found by parcel address on the county map's 2023 to 2025 justice precinct layer, not by city name. Mesa alone spans four precincts. In 2026 an eviction costs $69 to file and a writ of restitution $125, plus service; small claims costs $58 and is capped at $5,000. After move-out, a money case follows civil venue, usually the former tenant's address.

East Valley Justice Courts: Which One Hears Your Case

In Maricopa County, an eviction is filed in the justice court precinct that contains the rental property, not the precinct named on the mailing address. The East Valley is split across several precincts, and a single city can fall into two or three of them (Arizona Judicial Branch). The table shows where addresses in each city usually land. It is a starting point, not a substitute for an address lookup.

Queen Creek and Apache Junction are where the county line causes the most trouble. A Queen Creek or Apache Junction mailing address can sit in either county, and Pinal County runs a separate court system with its own fees. Our Pinal County guide covers that side of the line. Once you know the county, the next step is the precinct, and the county's own maps currently disagree with each other.

Find the Right Precinct Before You File

Maricopa County's Justice Courts tell filers to use the 2023 to 2025 justice precinct layer on the county's interactive elections map, not the 2026 or 2027 layers. The court posted that instruction on January 6, 2026 (Maricopa County Justice Courts). The newer map layer describes 27 precincts, while the courts still operate 26 (county ArcGIS item), so a lookup on the wrong layer can point you to the wrong court.

  1. Confirm the county. Use the parcel number and physical address, not the postal city.
  2. Look up the precinct. Use the elections map with the 2023 to 2025 justice precinct layer selected.
  3. Save the proof. Keep a screenshot or PDF of the result in the file.
  4. Call on border addresses. Confirm with the named court before paying the filing fee.
  5. Recheck after move-out. A money case filed later follows civil venue rules, which can point somewhere else.

In other words, the landlord pays for a wrong-precinct filing. The checklist further down tracks these steps for you. Once you have the right court, the costs are the same countywide.

How Much Does It Cost to File in Maricopa County?

An eviction (special detainer) complaint costs $69 to file in any Maricopa County justice court in 2026, and a small claims complaint costs $58 (Maricopa County fee schedule). These are all-in county figures. Arizona's statute lists a lower $35 base fee for a forcible detainer filing (A.R.S. 22-281), and Maricopa adds the authorized state and county surcharges on top.

Service is billed separately. It can be done by a constable, the sheriff or a registered private process server, and Arizona lets deposits and mileage be collected apart from the filing fee (A.R.S. 22-281). One county sheriff packet lists $16 for service plus $2.40 per one-way mile for each attempt and an $8 notary charge (Maricopa County Superior Court), but get a current quote from your precinct's constable or server rather than budgeting from that.

Watch for stale paperwork. An older Maricopa eviction instruction sheet still lists the writ of restitution at $115 (older PDF), while the 2026 schedule says $125. Use the fee page. The county also plans eFileAZ portal charges for justice court cases, including $6.50 to start a case and a 3% processing fee, as an extra cost on top of filing fees once the rollout arrives (Maricopa eFiling). With the costs known, timing is the other thing to plan around.

Eviction Timeline From Notice to Writ

An uncontested nonpayment eviction in the East Valley can reach judgment about 9 to 12 days after the five-day notice is delivered, and the landlord can usually get the writ about five days after that (Arizona Supreme Court eviction timeline). Service problems, contested hearings and constable workload all stretch that.

None of this is different from central Phoenix: the deadlines are statewide and the fees are countywide. We found no official source showing East Valley courts are faster or slower than the rest of the county. What changes is the money side after the resident leaves.

After Move-Out: Small Claims, Civil Court or Collections

Once the resident is gone, the balance becomes a money claim, and the court is chosen differently. Maricopa says small claims venue usually follows the defendant's address or the place where the transaction happened (Maricopa County Justice Courts). For a resident who moved from Tempe to Gilbert, that may not be the court that handled the eviction.

  • Small claims covers balances up to $5,000. Attorneys can't appear unless both sides agree, and there is no appeal.
  • Justice court civil covers claims up to $10,000. Above that, the case goes to Superior Court (Maricopa County Superior Court).
  • Small claims timing: service within 45 days, 20 days to answer, and a hearing within 60 days of the answer. The state's target is 75% of small claims cases finished within 100 days (Arizona Judicial Branch).

The hard part is rarely the filing. It's finding a current address to serve and an asset to collect from after you win. Mesa, Chandler, Gilbert and Tempe Local Rules

Arizona cities can't run their own general rental licensing for long-term rentals. A 2023 state law, Chapter 204, bars city-level long-term rental registration and ended city sales tax on residential leases of 30 days or more from January 1, 2025 (Laws 2023, Ch. 204; Arizona Department of Revenue). County assessor rental registration still applies.

  • Mesa: no separate city license for an ordinary long-term rental; the city's licensing page focuses on short-term rentals (City of Mesa).
  • Chandler: the city's rental page still mentions a residential rental tax license, but the state cancelled residential-only TPT licenses effective December 31, 2024 (City of Chandler; ADOR notice). Treat the city page as out of date.
  • Gilbert: no city rental licensing layer under A.R.S. 9-1304. Gilbert refers tenants in crisis to AZCEND for rent help (Town of Gilbert).
  • Tempe: source-of-income protection since Ordinance O2023.58 (November 30, 2023). Landlords can't reject an otherwise qualified applicant because rent comes through a Housing Choice Voucher, Social Security or veterans' benefits, and "no Section 8" ads are out (City of Tempe).

Tempe's rule matters for collections too. Apply the same screening, enforcement and move-out standards to voucher and non-voucher residents, and document the reason for every charge. Our guide to collecting unpaid rent from Section 8 tenants covers the voucher side. Assistance money is the other local factor.

Rental help in the East Valley is small and targeted. Mesa served 171 households with HOME-funded tenant-based rental assistance in FY2024/25 (Mesa CAPER), Chandler's program covers about 26 households (City of Chandler), and Tempe's Landlord Promise Program includes damage-claim help for voucher landlords (City of Tempe). Maricopa County's August 2026 eviction prevention pilot covers four Phoenix ZIP codes only, none of them in the East Valley (Maricopa County Justice Courts).

What Drives Move-Out Balances in the East Valley

Three things shape East Valley balances more than anywhere else in the metro: a huge student market in Tempe, a build-to-rent wave in the southeast, and heavy new apartment supply.

ASU and Tempe student leases

ASU projected more than 55,500 students at its Tempe campus for fall 2025, and nearly 13,000 moved into campus residence halls (ASU News; ASU News). The rest rent off campus, so turnover bunches around August move-in and May graduation. ASU says most nearby apartments use 12-month leases and that landlords commonly require a parent or guardian co-signer for students under 23 (ASU Off-Campus Housing). A balance with a signed guaranty is far more collectible than one without, so get the guarantor's address and phone at signing and handle roommate swaps and early exits with a written release, not a returned key.

Build-to-rent in Queen Creek, Mesa and Gilbert

A January 2025 analysis of communities with at least 50 single-family rentals counted 1,009 build-to-rent units in the Queen Creek pipeline, 419 in Mesa, 367 in Apache Junction, 162 in Chandler and 102 in Gilbert (In Business Phoenix, citing Point2 and Yardi Matrix). Single-family move-outs carry more charge types than apartments: landscaping, irrigation, pet damage, garage remotes, bigger utility bills and HOA violations. Photograph inside and out, keep HOA notices, and separate rent from damage in the final ledger so the undisputed part can be collected even if the damage is contested. Our guide to security deposits and move-out charges covers the itemization.

New apartment supply

Large projects under construction at the end of 2025 included the 419-unit Mason in East Mesa and the 722-unit Shorehaven and 586-unit Pier 202 in downtown Tempe (Kidder Mathews, Q4 2025). More supply and concessions give residents more places to go, so collect forwarding addresses, guarantor details and employer information before peak turnover, not after keys come back.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • Can I file a Mesa eviction in any East Valley justice court?

    No. It has to be filed in the precinct that contains the rental. If you file in the wrong one, the judge can transfer the case and the plaintiff pays the extra fees.

  • Do I need a city rental license in Mesa, Chandler or Gilbert?

    Not for an ordinary long-term rental. Arizona's 2023 Chapter 204 bars cities from general long-term rental licensing, and the state ended city sales tax on residential leases of 30 days or more in 2025. County assessor rental registration still applies.

  • Can a Tempe landlord refuse Section 8 vouchers?

    No. Tempe's Ordinance O2023.58 bars rejecting an otherwise qualified applicant because rent is paid through a Housing Choice Voucher or another lawful income source, and ads like "no Section 8" violate it.

  • Can I collect an ASU student's unpaid rent from a parent?

    Only if the parent signed a guaranty or co-signed the lease. ASU says landlords commonly require a co-signer for students under 23, so get a separately signed guaranty that covers rent, damage and collection costs.

  • What is the small claims limit in Maricopa County?

    $5,000. Claims up to $10,000 can go to the justice court's regular civil side, and anything larger goes to Superior Court. Attorneys can't appear in small claims unless both sides agree.

  • Is there rental assistance in Mesa or Chandler that can pay a tenant's back rent?

    Only small, targeted programs. Mesa served 171 households through tenant-based rental assistance in FY2024/25, and AZCEND handles crisis rent help for Chandler and Gilbert. Maricopa County's 2026 eviction prevention pilot covers four Phoenix ZIP codes, none in the East Valley.

  • Does a Queen Creek address file in Maricopa or Pinal County?

    It depends on the parcel. Queen Creek and Apache Junction both straddle the county line, so check the county first. Maricopa-side Queen Creek usually lands in the San Tan precinct; the Pinal side files in Pinal's justice courts.
