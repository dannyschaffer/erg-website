Collecting Unpaid Rent in Corpus Christi, TX: A Property Manager's Guide

Texas law sets the statewide rules, covered in our Texas rent collection laws guide. Corpus Christi adds four local wrinkles: nine justice courts spread across five precincts, constable fees the county doesn't publish clearly, a large military population, and a hurricane season that can end a lease mid-term. This is general information, not legal advice; confirm current fees with the court before filing.

Shawn Juarez, Managing Director, US Collections and Growth · Published October 8, 2026
Quick answer

Corpus Christi evictions go to whichever of Nueces County's nine justice of the peace courts covers the rental's precinct. The 2026 filing fee is $54 for an eviction or small claim, but current constable service and writ fees aren't in any accessible official schedule, so get them from the clerk or constable before filing. Texas's 2026 rules set the hearing 10 to 21 days after filing. Once the resident has left, collect the balance as a small claim up to $20,000. Check SCRA status on every file, since NAS Corpus Christi has no barracks for unaccompanied personnel. After a storm, separate rent owed before a casualty-loss termination from rent that stopped.

Nueces County's Nine Justice Courts

Venue follows the rental's precinct, and Nueces County has more courts than most Texas counties its size.

Nueces County splits five justice precincts into nine JP courts, listed in the county's JP directory. Use the county's JP and constable precinct maps to place the property rather than choosing the closest courthouse.

Some older county pages still say JP civil jurisdiction tops out at $10,000. The current limit under the Texas rules is $20,000, not counting interest and court costs, so treat the older figure as stale. With the court identified, the filing is standard Texas procedure.

Filing an Eviction in Corpus Christi

File in the precinct where the unit sits, with a ledger that separates every type of charge.

Nueces County takes JP filings electronically, in person or by mail, but the public eFileTexas list doesn't consistently show all nine courts, so confirm the intake method with your court. An e-filing provider may add its own fee. A solid packet includes:

  • A petition naming every tenant you need possession from.
  • The lease and addenda, plus the notice to vacate and proof of delivery.
  • A ledger that separates rent, late fees, utilities, damage, credits and payments.
  • Full legal names and service addresses for every defendant.
  • An SCRA military-status check before any default.

Under Texas's 2026 eviction rules, the trial date has to fall 10 to 21 days after filing, and trial can't happen until four days after service (Texas Supreme Court order, 2025). Two to three weeks to the first hearing is a fair plan for an uncontested case, but Nueces County doesn't publish a median, and filing in the wrong precinct, bad addresses, failed service, military status, jury requests or disputed storm-damage accounting all add time. The fees are the other thing to sort out before filing.

Once possession is resolved, the balance moves to a different kind of case.

Collecting the Balance After Move-Out

A money-only claim against a former resident is a small-claims case, and it moves on the court's schedule, not a fixed timeline.

The specialized Texas debt-claim category is aimed at lenders, debt collectors and assignees, so a landlord suing on its own lease generally files small claims. The defendant has until the end of the 14th day after service to answer, and the judge sets trial after that (Texas State Law Library). Nueces County publishes no court-wide median for these cases, so don't count on a 30-day or six-week result. That's one reason many owners place former-resident balances with an unpaid rent collection agency instead of waiting on a docket. In Corpus Christi, a military check comes before either path.

NAS Corpus Christi and the SCRA

Check military status on every file before asking for a default, because many local servicemembers rent off base.

NAS Corpus Christi hosts naval aviation training, the Corpus Christi Army Depot and many tenant commands. The Navy's housing office says accompanied personnel may rent in the community and notes the base has no unaccompanied barracks or dormitories (CNIC Navy Housing). Before a default judgment, you have to file an affidavit on whether the defendant is in military service. If they appear to be, the court generally can't enter default until it appoints an attorney, and the case may be stayed for at least 90 days (U.S. Department of Justice). Run the DoD SCRA status check close to the hearing date; a "not military" box on the application isn't enough.

A servicemember with permanent change of station orders or a deployment of at least 90 days can end the lease with written notice and the orders. For monthly rent, termination takes effect 30 days after the next rent due date. Stop future rent on that date and refund any prepaid rent after it, but keep the legitimate arrears from before; no early-termination penalty is allowed. Storms create a similar split between what stays owed and what stops.

Hurricanes and Casualty-Loss Balances

A storm warning doesn't cancel rent. A casualty-loss termination stops future rent but leaves earlier rent owed.

Hurricane season runs June 1 through November 30. Under Texas Property Code 92.054, if a unit becomes totally unusable for residential purposes through no fault of the tenant's household, either side can end the lease in writing before repairs are finished. The tenant then gets prorated rent back from the move-out date plus the deposit refund the law already requires. If the unit is only partly unusable, a rent reduction takes a county or district court judgment unless the lease says otherwise, and for insured damage the repair clock doesn't start until the landlord receives the insurance proceeds. Texas disaster guidance confirms that rent and charges owed before a valid termination can still be collected (TexasLawHelp).

Before placing any post-storm balance with an agency, document the damage date and cause, whether the unit was totally or partly unusable, any alleged tenant fault, the termination notice date, the actual move-out date, prepaid rent, insurance proceeds and repair dates, and any written rent-reduction agreement. The wider market shapes how much of any balance is worth chasing.

Corpus Christi's student market is smaller than Austin's but runs on the same August-to-May cycle.

For those leases, use guaranties that name exactly what they cover, state whether roommates are jointly liable or on individual bedroom leases, keep move-in condition evidence for damage allocation, and don't assume that withdrawing or graduating ends a private lease. That leads to the last step: turning a judgment into money.

Collecting a JP Judgment in Nueces County

Record an abstract for a real-property lien, then use a writ of execution for non-exempt personal property.

  • Abstract of judgment. Once the judgment is final, get an abstract from the JP clerk for $5 and record it with the Nueces County Clerk, and in any other county where the debtor owns real estate. The judgment alone doesn't create the lien (Texas Justice Court Training Center).
  • Writ of execution. Generally available 30 days after final judgment if no appeal or supersedeas blocks it. The clerk charges $5 per page; constable levy, storage, advertising and sale costs are separate, so get a written estimate first (Texas State Law Library).
  • Release. After payment in full, file a release wherever you recorded the abstract.

Texas exemptions protect a lot, including homesteads and wages, which is why many judgments end up with an agency. Key takeaways
  • Nueces County has nine JP courts in five precincts. File in the precinct where the rental sits, using the county's precinct maps.
  • The 2026 filing fee is $54. Constable service and writ fees are set locally and not clearly published, so confirm them before filing.
  • Ignore the $104 filing figure on some commercial sites and any $10,000 JP limit on older county pages; the limit is $20,000.
  • Texas's 2026 rules put the eviction hearing 10 to 21 days after filing; money-only cases have no reliable timeline.
  • Run a DoD SCRA status check before every default. NAS Corpus Christi personnel commonly rent off base.
  • A hurricane doesn't cancel rent by itself. Under Property Code 92.054, rent owed before a valid casualty termination stays collectible; future rent stops.
  • To collect a JP judgment, record a $5 abstract for a real-property lien and get a constable's cost estimate before requesting a writ of execution.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • How much does it cost to file an eviction in Corpus Christi?

    The court filing fee is $54 in 2026. Constable service and any writ fee are extra and set locally; get the current amounts from the Nueces County JP clerk or constable before filing.

  • Which court handles evictions in Corpus Christi?

    One of Nueces County's nine justice of the peace courts. Five sit in Corpus Christi itself, and the right one depends on which precinct the rental property is in.

  • How long does an eviction take in Nueces County?

    Under Texas's 2026 rules the trial is set 10 to 21 days after filing, so two to three weeks to the first hearing is a reasonable plan for an uncontested case. Nueces County doesn't publish a median.

  • Does a hurricane mean a tenant doesn't owe rent?

    Not automatically. If the unit becomes totally unusable through no fault of the household, either side can end the lease in writing. Rent accrued before that termination is still owed; rent after it stops and prepaid rent is refunded.

  • Can a servicemember at NAS Corpus Christi break a lease early?

    Yes, under the SCRA, with qualifying orders and written notice. Termination generally takes effect 30 days after the next rent due date. Rent owed before then stays collectible, but an early-termination fee doesn't.

  • Should I file a small claim or a debt claim for a former tenant's balance?

    A small claim. Texas debt claims are meant for lenders, collection agencies and debt buyers. A landlord suing on its own lease usually files small claims, up to $20,000.

  • How do I collect on a JP judgment in Nueces County?

    Get an abstract of judgment from the JP clerk ($5) and record it with the county clerk to create a lien on non-exempt real property. After 30 days you can request a writ of execution; the clerk charges $5 per page and the constable's costs are separate.
