Collecting Unpaid Rent in Tucson, AZ: A Property Manager's Guide

Arizona's landlord-tenant act and collection rules set the statewide floor, covered in our Arizona debt collection laws guide. This is what's different in Tucson: one consolidated justice court instead of Maricopa's 26 precincts, a writ fee the county publishes two different ways, a city source-of-income ordinance, a university rental cycle that turns over every summer, and an Air Force base. This is general information, not legal advice; confirm current fees with the court before filing.

Shawn Juarez, Managing Director, US Collections and Growth · Published October 1, 2026
Quick answer

Tucson-area evictions and civil claims go to the Pima County Consolidated Justice Court. As of 2026, an eviction complaint costs $71, a regular civil complaint $116, and a small-claims complaint $60. The court sets the first eviction hearing 3 to 6 business days after filing, and an ordinary writ of restitution can issue five days after judgment, so a clean case reaches writ eligibility in roughly 8 to 13 days. Once the resident has moved out, collect the balance through small claims (up to $5,000) or a regular civil suit (up to $10,000), not an eviction. Tucson's source-of-income ordinance affects screening, not the rent claim. Near the University of Arizona, match every demand to the lease structure and guarantor, and near Davis-Monthan, check SCRA status before any default.

Choosing the Right Case in Pima County

The eviction track is for getting the unit back. Once possession isn't at issue, the balance belongs in a money case.

Don't file an eviction just to collect money from someone who has already left. The Pima County Consolidated Justice Court at 240 N. Stone handles Tucson-area evictions and limited-jurisdiction civil cases in one place, which makes venue simpler than in Maricopa County.

Filing an Eviction in Tucson

Pima's packet asks for more ledger history than many landlords expect.

Use the county's eviction complaint and summons packet. Each named defendant must be served with the complaint, summons, the Residential Eviction Information Sheet and the lease, and for nonpayment the packet also requires an accounting of charges and payments for the preceding six months. Service has to be done by a constable or a registered private process server, and proof of service must reach the court before the hearing.

The clerk sets the first hearing 3 to 6 business days after filing. If the case is decided that day, judgment comes within about a week. An ordinary writ of restitution can then issue five days after judgment, or within 12 to 24 hours for an irreparable breach. The landlord has to request the writ, and only a constable can execute it. On paper, that's roughly 8 to 13 calendar days from filing to writ eligibility, plus the constable's queue. Pima doesn't publish a median from filing to lockout, and continuances, service problems, bankruptcy, SCRA relief or an appeal all extend it.

Filing a Debt Case After Move-Out

The money track runs on answer and default deadlines, so it takes weeks, not days.

  • Regular civil (up to $10,000). File the civil complaint packet and serve every defendant within 90 days. A defendant served in Arizona has 20 days to answer, or 30 if served out of state. If there's no answer, apply for default, mail it, wait ten business days, then request default judgment with the lease, ledger, invoices, photos and deposit accounting. Most defaults are decided without a hearing.
  • Small claims (up to $5,000). Can be started online. The defendant has 20 days to answer, and if they do, the court sets a hearing within 60 days.

Even uncontested, a debt case can't reach default judgment until the 20-day answer period and the ten-business-day default period have both run. A contested small claim can take the service period plus up to 80 more days. That's the main reason to place a move-out balance with an agency early, before addresses go stale.

Tucson's Source-of-Income Ordinance

This rule changes how you screen and advertise. It doesn't change what a resident owes.

Inside city limits, Tucson's housing discrimination rules protect lawful source of income. Landlords can't reject an applicant solely because income comes from a Housing Choice Voucher, rapid rehousing, or other public assistance, and the definition also covers child support, Social Security, disability and veterans' benefits. According to the Arizona Attorney General's review, the city's position is that the ordinance doesn't force rent reductions, dictate eviction terms, or require continued leasing to a voucher holder who doesn't pay.

In practice: apply the same written screening formula to every applicant, count lawful assistance consistently, remove any "no Section 8" language, and make sure every collection or eviction decision rests on the ledger or a lease breach. For what you can and can't recover from a voucher tenant, see our guide to collecting unpaid rent from Section 8 tenants.

Eviction Prevention and Rental Assistance

More Tucson tenants show up with help, and that can work in your favor.

Pima County's Emergency Eviction Legal Services provides navigation for landlords and tenants and free counsel to tenants at or below 80% of area median income. The county reports it has served more than 7,300 households since 2021, and its FY2027 budget continued funding. Expect a navigator or tenant lawyer to ask for documentation and time to arrange assistance. A verified payment commitment often recovers more than a judgment against someone with nothing to collect.

The large pandemic-era rental assistance program is closed. Current county help for rent and utilities is limited, grant-dependent and not guaranteed. The city reported 19 households receiving $60,971.85 through its Eviction Prevention Grant in 2025, so treat assistance as targeted, not a general arrears fund.

University of Arizona Rentals

Student leases change who owes what, and the summer exodus changes how fast you need to act.

Most Tucson apartments use 12-month leases, but properties near campus also offer 9-, 10- and 11-month terms, individual by-the-bedroom leases and roommate matching. The student cycle shows up in the data too: PICOR reported the University submarket's sharpest occupancy drop in Q2 2025, tied to student turnover.

  • Match the demand to the lease. An individual lease isolates each student's rent and damage. A joint lease makes every tenant liable for the full balance only if the contract clearly says so. Don't split or combine roommate balances by default.
  • Get a real guaranty. A separately signed guaranty should cover rent, utilities, damage, renewals, holdover, collection costs and post-move-out charges. A parent listed as an emergency contact isn't a guarantor.
  • Keep bedroom-level evidence. Inspection photos, move-in condition forms and written allocation agreements, especially for common-area damage on individual leases.
  • Move before May. Collect forwarding and permanent home addresses, guarantor contacts and inspection evidence before the mid-May move-out wave. Send the final ledger to every liable student and guarantor at once, and file before graduates or international students leave Arizona.

Davis-Monthan and the SCRA

Screen every file for military status before seeking a default or a lockout.

Under the Servicemembers Civil Relief Act, a servicemember who signed before entering service, or who later gets permanent change of station or deployment orders of at least 90 days, can end a residential lease with written notice and a copy of the orders. For monthly rent, termination takes effect 30 days after the next rent due date. You can collect prorated rent and legitimate charges due before then, including excess wear, but not an early-termination penalty or a disguised concession recapture.

The SCRA also bars eviction of a protected servicemember or dependent without a court order and can require a 90-day stay when service affects the ability to pay. Keep the orders, the notice, the effective-date calculation and the inspection evidence in the file, and before any default, use the Defense Department's status check and file the military-status affidavit. A Davis-Monthan address or an application checkbox isn't enough.

Key takeaways
  • Tucson-area cases go to one court, the Pima County Consolidated Justice Court.
  • 2026 fees: $71 eviction, $116 regular civil, $60 small claims. The writ issuance fee is published as both $38 and $43; confirm with the clerk.
  • A clean eviction reaches writ eligibility in roughly 8 to 13 days; a debt case takes weeks because of answer and default periods.
  • Collect a post-move-out balance through small claims or regular civil, not an eviction.
  • Tucson's source-of-income ordinance governs screening and advertising, not the rent claim.
  • For student rentals, match every demand to the lease structure and a signed guaranty, and act before the May move-out wave.
  • Check SCRA status before any default, and don't charge an early-termination fee on a valid SCRA termination.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • How much does it cost to file an eviction in Tucson?

    As of 2026 the eviction complaint fee at the Pima County Consolidated Justice Court is $71. A writ of restitution adds an issuance fee the county publishes as either $38 or $43, a $10 constable surcharge, and the constable's execution charge.

  • How fast is an eviction in Pima County?

    The court sets the first hearing 3 to 6 business days after filing, and an ordinary writ of restitution can issue five days after judgment. A clean case reaches writ eligibility in roughly 8 to 13 calendar days, plus the constable's scheduling.

  • Can I use an eviction case to collect from a tenant who already moved out?

    No. Once possession isn't at issue, file a small claim for balances up to $5,000 or a regular civil case up to $10,000. Balances above $10,000 go to Pima County Superior Court.

  • Does Tucson's source-of-income ordinance stop me from evicting a voucher tenant?

    No. It bars rejecting applicants because of lawful income sources like vouchers. You can still enforce the lease and collect the tenant's share, as long as the decision rests on the ledger or a lease breach.

  • Are parents liable for a University of Arizona student's unpaid rent?

    Only if they signed a guaranty that covers it. Being listed as an emergency contact or paying rent informally doesn't make a parent liable.

  • Can a Davis-Monthan servicemember break a lease without a penalty?

    Yes, under the SCRA, with qualifying orders and written notice. You can collect rent and legitimate charges that accrued before the termination took effect, but not an early-termination fee.
