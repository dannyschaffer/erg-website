Collecting Unpaid Rent in Sugar Land, Katy & Missouri City, TX: A Property Manager's Guide to Fort Bend County

Texas law sets the rules statewide, covered in our Texas rent collection laws guide. Collecting unpaid rent in Sugar Land, Katy and Missouri City adds three local traps: Fort Bend redrew its justice precincts for 2026, two of its biggest cities spill into Harris and Waller counties, and its suburban rental stock brings HOA rules and storm damage into the ledger. This is general information, not legal advice; confirm fees with the court before filing.

Fort Bend County's Seven Justice Courts

Fort Bend County now runs seven justice of the peace courts across four precincts, and the precinct lines changed on January 1, 2026. Precincts 1, 2 and 3 each have two courts (called places) and Precinct 4 has one, after a new Precinct 3, Place 2 court opened in Needville in May 2026 (Covering Katy, 2026).

The county adopted new justice-precinct boundaries in late 2025, effective January 1, 2026 (Fort Bend Commissioners Court order, 2025). That means a precinct you used for the same building in 2025, or an old property-management record, is not proof of where to file today. The bigger trap, though, is the county line itself.

Katy and Missouri City Cross County Lines

The city on the mailing address doesn't decide the court. The physical location of the rental does. Katy spans Harris, Fort Bend and Waller counties, and Missouri City spans Fort Bend and Harris, so the same city name can send you to three different county court systems.

Harris County spells out the rule plainly: an eviction is filed in the justice precinct where the premises are located (Harris County Justice Courts). Fort Bend's courts and constables use the county's voter street guide to confirm jurisdiction on service addresses (Fort Bend JP 4). To get it right every time:

  1. Find the county. Search the parcel in the Fort Bend, Harris or Waller appraisal district.
  2. Find the precinct. Use Fort Bend's current street guide or 2026 precinct map, not a ZIP code.
  3. Confirm the place. In Precincts 1 to 3, call a clerk or check which court eFileTexas offers.
  4. Save the proof. Keep a screenshot of the parcel and precinct lookup in the file in case venue is challenged.

Filing by the landlord's address, the management office or the tenant's new address risks a transfer or dismissal and a second round of service fees. The checklist further down tracks these steps for you. Once you have the right court, the fees are predictable.

How Much Does an Eviction Cost in Fort Bend County?

A one-defendant eviction costs $134 to file in Fort Bend: a $54 court fee plus $80 for constable service (Fort Bend eviction packet, 2026). Each extra defendant adds another $80, and the constable charges $180 to execute a writ of possession.

Fees are only half the planning, though. The 2026 Texas rules also tightened the clock.

Eviction Timeline Under the 2026 Texas Rules

An uncontested Fort Bend eviction usually takes about three to five weeks from filing to getting the unit back. For cases filed from January 1, 2026, the trial must be set 10 to 21 days after filing, and no earlier than the fourth day after service (Tex. Prop. Code ch. 24).

  • The constable must make a diligent effort to serve within five business days of filing (Texas Supreme Court rules order).
  • A continuance is usually limited to seven days unless both sides agree in writing.
  • The writ generally can't issue before the sixth day after judgment, and the constable must serve it within five business days.

That window doesn't include the notice to vacate before filing, service problems, agreed continuances or appeals. Our Texas rent collection law guide covers the statewide notice and appeal rules. The eviction gets the unit back, but if the tenant has already left, you're filing a different kind of case.

After Move-Out: Small Claims, Not a Debt Claim

A property owner suing a former tenant for unpaid rent or damages files a small-claims case, not a debt claim. Fort Bend reserves debt claims for assignees, debt collectors and collection agencies, financial institutions and lenders (Fort Bend JP debt claims). The small-claims limit is $20,000, including attorney fees but not interest or court costs.

Collecting Unpaid Rent in Sugar Land, Katy and Missouri City: Local Rules and Assistance

We found no active 2026 city or county program in Fort Bend that pays ordinary rent arrears to landlords, and no 2024 to 2026 ordinance in Sugar Land, Katy, Missouri City, Rosenberg or Richmond that changes the justice court process for a standard residential lease.

The programs that do exist are narrow. Fort Bend residents could apply for FEMA help after Hurricane Beryl in 2024 (Fort Bend County, 2024). HUD's $104,420 ESG-RUSH award went to shelter renovation, not rent arrears (HUD, 2024), and the state's disaster recovery programs cover owner-occupied homes. Point tenants to the justice court clerk, county social services and 211 Texas rather than promising help that may not exist. City rental-registration and short-term-rental rules are a separate compliance question from the court process. What does vary locally is the kind of housing you're managing.

Build-to-Rent, HOAs and Energy-Sector Turnover

Fort Bend's rental stock is increasingly suburban single-family homes inside master-planned communities, and that changes what's collectible. Katy and Fulshear had 12 build-to-rent communities of 56 to 266 homes in a 2025 count, with another 798 build-to-rent homes projected to be occupied from 2024 to 2028 (Community Impact, 2025).

New build-to-rent communities compete on amenities and concessions, so compare effective rent, not advertised rent, and check whether the lease lets you recover a concession when a tenant breaks it early. Master-planned communities add a private layer too. Cinco Ranch, for example, requires a signed Notice of Tenant Residency form, a copy of the lease and ID before tenants get amenity access (Cinco Residential). An HOA fine isn't rent: whether you can collect it from the tenant depends on the lease and the governing documents, and lumping it into the rent ledger can complicate an otherwise simple nonpayment case.

Employment adds turnover risk. Fort Bend is tied to Houston's energy and engineering economy, and 53% of working-age residents commuted outside the county for work in 2024 (Fort Bend Economic Development, 2024). Corporate transfers can mean early move-outs with employer-paid lease breaks, so get forwarding addresses, relocation-company details and a written surrender agreement quickly. Storms are the other local risk that can change a balance.

Hurricane and Flood Damage Can Change What's Owed

After a storm, Texas Property Code 92.054 can cut the balance you're allowed to collect. If a casualty makes the home practically unusable and the tenant didn't cause it, either side can end the lease, and the tenant gets prorated rent from move-out and the deposit back (Tex. Prop. Code 92.054). If the home is only partly unusable, the tenant can get a proportional rent reduction through a court judgment unless the lease says otherwise.

The risk is real in Fort Bend. About 20.5% of the county's land is in the 100-year flood zone, and Hurricane Beryl dropped 14.99 inches of rain at Thompsons in 2024 (Fort Bend County, 2024). For any storm-affected account, keep the move-out date, written termination, adjuster report, insurance payment date, repair invoices and any rent-abatement agreement. A demand that ignores a lawful termination or refund overstates the balance, and an overstated balance is exactly what triggers disputes once an account goes to collections. Frequently Asked Questions

  • Where do I file an eviction in Sugar Land?

    In the Fort Bend justice precinct that contains the property, which for much of Sugar Land is Precinct 3, Place 1 at 151 Stadium Drive. Check the exact address on the county's 2026 street guide first, because precinct lines changed on January 1, 2026.

  • Which county court handles a Katy rental property?

    It depends on the parcel. Katy spans Harris, Fort Bend and Waller counties, so search the address on the appraisal district site, then file in that county's justice precinct where the property sits.

  • How much does it cost to file an eviction in Fort Bend County?

    $134 for one defendant: a $54 court fee plus $80 for constable service. Each additional defendant adds $80, a jury costs $22, and executing a writ of possession costs $180 for the constable plus the clerk's issuance fee.

  • How long does an eviction take in Fort Bend County?

    Usually about three to five weeks from filing to possession if uncontested. The 2026 Texas rules set trial 10 to 21 days after filing, and the writ generally can't issue until the sixth day after judgment, then must be served within five business days.

  • Can I sue a former tenant in Fort Bend after they move out?

    Yes, in small claims for up to $20,000, including attorney fees. An owner shouldn't file a debt claim, which Fort Bend reserves for assignees, collection agencies, lenders and financial institutions. Venue starts from where the former tenant now lives.

  • Does Fort Bend County have an eviction diversion or rent assistance program?

    We found no active 2026 city or county program that pays ordinary rent arrears to landlords. Post-Beryl aid went to FEMA individual assistance, shelters and owner-occupied homes. Refer tenants to the court clerk, county social services or 211 Texas.

  • Can I charge a tenant for HOA fines in Fort Bend?

    Only if the lease and the community's governing documents make the tenant responsible. Keep HOA fines separate from rent in the ledger; mixing them into a nonpayment eviction can complicate the case.

  • Is Fort Bend County faster than Harris County for evictions?

    Not reliably. Both follow the same 2026 statewide timetable, and neither publishes a median time to lockout. Fort Bend is $5 cheaper to file for one defendant but $25 more for the constable's writ.
