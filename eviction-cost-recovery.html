Eviction cost recovery

Eviction Cost Recovery for Property Portfolios

An eviction ends the lease, but it rarely ends the debt. Court costs, legal fees, and the money judgment itself often go uncollected after the case closes, and pursuing that judgment is a different job than pursuing the original rent balance.

Book a Recovery Review

What counts as eviction cost recovery.

A judgment is a legal win, not a recovery. Collecting on it is a separate step most property teams don't have the process to pursue.

Court and filing costs

The eviction filing, service of process, and court fees the property advanced.

Attorney and legal fees

Legal costs incurred pursuing the eviction, separate from the underlying rent balance.

Judgment balance

The money judgment the court awarded that the former resident never paid.

Lost rent through the process

Rent that accrued on the unit while the eviction moved through the court.

Frequently asked

Eviction cost recovery questions, answered.

  • What does eviction cost recovery include?

    Court and filing costs, attorney and legal fees, the unpaid money judgment, and rent lost while the eviction moved through the court: the full cost of the eviction, not just the original rent balance.

  • Is a court judgment the same as getting paid?

    No. A judgment establishes what's owed legally, but collecting the money owed is a separate step, one most property teams don't have an in-house process for.

  • Can ERG collect on a judgment that's already been through court?

    Yes. ERG places and pursues post-judgment accounts the same way it handles any other move-out or aging balance, under the same compliance standards.

  • What states can ERG recover eviction costs in?

    AZ, OR, TX, UT, and WA today; four more states are in progress as part of the 2026 expansion.
